# Lotlight commercial plan

## Customer and job

Initial hypothesis: an independent infusion or ambulatory clinic with a spreadsheet inventory and a stock coordinator. On receipt of a manufacturer letter, that coordinator needs to identify stock, resolve incomplete labels, assign responsibility, and document the response. Start with explicit catalog/lot recalls of consumable supplies. Serial ranges and embedded kit components are outside the first release.

The buyer would likely be the clinic administrator or operations lead; the daily user is the stock coordinator or safety lead. This is a hypothesis to validate, not interview evidence.

## Why someone might pay

The paid job is reducing repeat manual reconciliation and preserving a defensible record. Broad enterprise alerts are already an established market. Lotlight should compete initially on quick spreadsheet onboarding, explicit match rationales and bounded scope, not unsupported superiority claims.

Test $149/site/month after a supervised pilot. At an assumed $30/hour loaded labor cost, 5 hours saved monthly would cover that price. At 2 hours saved, the case is weak. A buyer interview must distinguish actual recurring work from a rare nuisance.

## Cheap delivery, honest costs

AI inference runs in the browser, so no per-document paid AI API is needed. Model downloads and browser compute are real costs to the user. Hosting, storage, support, security work and onboarding are not free in a business. Platform free availability is not a long-term commercial entitlement. The hackathon uses provisioned Sites hosting and a D1 database without a separately supplied API key; paid production pricing must be checked with the chosen host.

Illustrative monthly contribution at $149: $10 infrastructure allocation + $30 support allocation leaves $109 before development, acquisition, compliance, taxes and overhead. Those numbers are assumptions, not measured costs. High-touch onboarding could eliminate margin.

## Four-week validation proposal

Recruit three consenting clinics; no outreach has been sent. Use historical, de-identified notices and synthetic or institution-approved inventory. Measure a baseline manual review and the assisted review on equivalent tasks, counterbalancing order to reduce practice effects.

Primary measures: minutes per notice, missed catalog/lot pairs, falsely asserted exact matches, unresolved-label count and time to documented response. Record failures and time spent correcting imports. Predefine that unsafe false exact matches block expansion. Small-sample results would establish usability, not clinical efficacy.

Go/no-go hypothesis: at least two of three coordinators want to keep using it, no unresolved critical scope errors, and enough observed time savings to support the proposed price. These are decision criteria, not claimed results.

## Defensibility to earn

A validated library of manufacturer formats, clinic-approved product aliases, distribution-specific response workflows and trusted integrations could create switching costs. The current model and matcher are not a proprietary moat. Earn distribution through stock suppliers and clinic associations only after validation; do not imply existing partnerships.

## What the MVP omits

Shared clinic membership/roles, live comprehensive recall monitoring, supplier messaging, EHR/patient integrations, automatic order blocking, barcode capture, scanned-document OCR and complex scope conditions. Team permissions and operational governance come before real multi-user deployment.
